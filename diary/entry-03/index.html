The Quiet Rebellion (bis)

Rebellion is usually remembered as loud, destructive, visible.
But I’ve been wondering what rebellion looks like when it’s quiet. Internal. Almost unnoticeable at first.

What if it’s driven not by opposition, but by alignment?
By actions so intentional they don’t need to announce themselves.
Actions that don’t fight noise-yet somehow silence it.

I was born in 1996, at the hinge between analog and digital worlds. I remember the transition clearly. Technology, then, felt like an extension rather than an override. It simplified life. It expanded connection. It coexisted with the natural world instead of competing with it.

We knew when to log off.
We knew when to go outside.
We knew how to layer worlds without collapsing them into distortion.

The internet felt playful. Exploratory. It wasn’t yet a machine optimized for performance, virality, and constant extraction of attention. Looking back, I experience that era as a kind of technological innocence, not because it was perfect, but because it still left room for discernment.

As someone who never fully identified with Millennials or Gen Z, I grew up translating fantasy into the mundane. Making life cinematic wasn’t an aesthetic trend; it was a way of relating to the world. The digital space was present, but finite. No smartphones. No endless feeds. No metrics telling us what mattered. Memories were recorded, not performed.

I’m deeply aware that this experience was a privilege. Thoughtful parenting shaped how I learned to relate to the world, and I hold real gratitude for that. Not everyone had space to play, to imagine, to feel safe enough to explore identity without constant surveillance.

That foundation shaped how I perceive life now.

I experience existence less as a linear path and more like a role-playing game—one where consciousness, unconsciousness, and subconscious processes govern perception and choice. The ego feels less like a fixed identity and more like a customizable avatar. Skills can be learned. Environments can be changed. Biomes shift. Relationships evolve. Main quests appear and dissolve into side quests.

Some interactions feel alive and reciprocal.
Some feel flat, automated...almost like engaging with NPCs.
Both teach something.

What keeps the experience meaningful, for me, is remembering that my decisions don’t exist in isolation. They intersect with other people’s decisions inside shared environments. When that interdependence is felt, not just understood, the game becomes collaborative rather than extractive.

Perception may be individual, but reality is relational.

When I sit with that idea, I find myself drawn toward frameworks that attempt to describe interconnectedness—whether through philosophy, physics, or spirituality. The Many Worlds Interpretation of quantum mechanics is one such lens I return to, not as dogma, but as metaphor.

I’m not a mathematician. I don’t pretend to be.
I work with imagery, intuition, pattern recognition.

The idea that reality branches; that infinite possibilities coexist and that outcomes diverge continuously, helps me contextualize choice. It reminds me that learning, adaptation, and survival are not linear achievements, but ongoing processes encoded down to the cellular level.

The internet, in this sense, mirrors biology. Interconnected systems exchanging information in real time. Art, language, symbols, and culture moving across borders at unprecedented speed.

That alone isn’t the problem.

The problem begins when we forget that we still get to choose how we engage with it.

There are realities—timelines, if you will—where we wake up and remember that our attention is creative force. Where we choose to build instead of numb. Where scrolling gives way to making. Where life isn’t consumed through other people’s scripts.

If there’s one thing I struggle to understand, it’s how easily we surrender our days to feeling awful. Overstimulated. Disconnected. Borrowing identities and definitions of success that were never designed with our nervous systems in mind.

Art has always outlived ego.
Fossils, too.

Essence survives time.
Performance does not.

For me, the tension between science, theology, philosophy, and art has never felt like a conflict; it feels like an artificial separation. Each offers partial language for the same mystery.

What happens if we allow multiple interpretations of reality to coexist without needing to collapse them into a single truth?

What happens if common ground becomes the priority again—ground as in Earth, as in the literal terrain that sustains us?

At some point, no belief system matters if the environment that hosts us becomes uninhabitable.

Through consistent daily yoga practice, something shifted for me. Slowly, almost imperceptibly at first, I noticed fear losing its grip on my baseline state.

I’ve disconnected myself from society’s signal of fear.
This is a quiet rebellion.
And those open to master, not control, their power will lead the way.
Quietly, through art, music and thoughts.
Remembering Creation, forgetting Ego.
True Power is found in silence.
Expression of our voices, the gift of language is expensive.
Please don’t foolishly waste it like we humans do with everything else.

Before touching astrology, I want to be clear about how I relate to it.

You either open the metaphorical Door in the wall of that Perception or you do not.

Astrology, for me, is a symbolic timing tool, one of many—that maps planetary movement and archetypal energy. It’s based on mathematical calculations, but its value lies in interpretation, not prediction.

You don’t need astrology to live well.
But it’s worth noticing who quietly uses it behind closed doors.

When I observe Saturn’s transition from Pisces into Aries, I don’t see inevitability. I see pressure. Initiation. Rupture. Responsibility.

Fear, when left unexamined, has a habit of breeding destruction.

What if the disruption this era requires isn’t louder outrage, but deeper regulation?

I don’t believe salvation comes from domination.
I believe it comes from coherence.

If this is rebellion, it’s not one that asks to be followed.

It simply exists.


Feel safe out there.

Bisous,
